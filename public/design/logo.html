Crest, seal, glyph

The Glyph

The S alone, scaled to fill. Wherever the ring would close into a dark rim: favicon, notifications, dense lists.

/brand/glyph-{64,128,256,512}.webp
1–20KB
The S meets the ring; it does not float inside it. The glyph source ships on an opaque cream field, so composited straight in it punched a square hole through the outline. That field is knocked out to transparency first, which then allows the S to be scaled to 0.90 of the canvas — its tips reaching the ring’s inner edge — instead of the 0.56 it sat at, where its ink reached only 64% of the usable radius and the seal read as mostly empty.
Why the outline is heavy, and why the earlier one failed. The first seal used two hairline rings at 3.4px and 2.2px on a 1024px canvas. At 44px — the size it is actually used — that renders as 0.26px: below one pixel, so it cannot draw a line and turns into a faint grey haze. It read as a circle-ish smudge rather than a pressed seal. The outline is now a single stroke at 24px, which is 1.03px at 44 — the lightest weight that still renders as a line rather than a smear — while the S is scaled so its tips meet the ring, the way they do in the crest. Its colour is #604020, sampled from the S itself (65% of the glyph’s ink), so the ring reads as the same material as the mark rather than a foreign circle drawn around it. Contrast on cream: 8.3:1. The S is also nudged +28px right: its ink mass measured 10% left of the geometric centre, so a mathematically centred placement looked visibly off.
The mark switch is automatic; the crest is a deliberate choice. <Mark size={n} /> picks the seal at 44 and above, the glyph below it, and selects the nearest export up from size × 2. The crest has no component and no small exports — it must be reached for on purpose, which is what stops it drifting into the interface.

02 · The crest

Why it is not the logo

The crest was chosen from eight candidates. It is the layered paper-cut version — dimensional depth that is a liability in a logo and an asset in a print piece. It is still the wrong object for a 44px slot — which is the whole reason the seal and glyph exist.

Read the 44px row carefully. The crest scores highest of the three, and the number is misleading in exactly the way it was throughout this process: it counts surviving contrast, not whether that contrast resolves into a shape anyone recognises. 04 · How it was made

Drawn glyph, generated frame

This split is the useful finding, and it took roughly a dozen rejected candidates to arrive at.

PartMade byBecause
The S, the copper ribbon, the cobbled roaddrawnOrganic calligraphic form. Generators are excellent at this and code is hopeless at it.
The ring, its concentric hairlinegenerated geometryExact circle from arithmetic. Generators get this subtly wrong every single time.
The measurement that proved it. The supplied seal artwork was checked before use: its ring measured 351 × 364 — an oval, 3.6% taller than wide, with the radius varying ±1.32%. Invisible in a nav bar, clearly out of round on a book cover. The design was kept and the geometry rebuilt: 382 × 382, aspect 1.0000.

06 · Rules

Do, and do not

✓Do

  • Use <Mark size={n} /> and let it choose
  • Mask to a disc on any dark ground
  • Pair with the wordmark until recognition exists
  • Keep clear space of at least half the mark’s width
  • Reach for the crest only at 96px and above

×Don’t

  • Recolour it, tint it, or make a per-section variant
  • Use the seal below 44px — the ring closes up
  • Stretch it; the ring is a true circle and must stay one
  • Reintroduce any of the retired marks
  • Put the crest anywhere in the interface
The favicon is a circle, not a square. CSS rounds the mark everywhere else, but the browser draws the favicon file exactly as given — so icon.png is masked to a disc with transparent corners, and the S is scaled to 90% of the radius so its extremes are not clipped by the mask. The Apple touch icon deliberately stays square: iOS applies its own rounding, and transparent corners there render as black.
One flaw still open. A faint vertical seam runs through the glyph where the automatic trim caught a slight variation in the source background. Invisible at interface sizes; it would show on a large print. Regenerate the glyph on a perfectly flat ground before it goes on a cover.
