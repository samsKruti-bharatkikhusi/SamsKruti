06 · Imagery

The product is mostly photographs

Everything above specifies type on colour. But Aklavya will spend most of his time looking at pictures of places, food and faces — so imagery needs the same rigour. All swatches below are CSS stand-ins, now the real photographs from /data/images/, including the bright daylight one, because that is the photograph that breaks scrims.

Four ratios, and no others

Fixed ratios stop the layout jumping as images load — the single biggest source of “cheap” feel in an image-heavy app. Each ratio has one job.

Three scrims, tested on the brightest real photo

Why the scrim is warm-black rgba(20,10,4,…), not neutral: a grey scrim over a warm photograph turns it muddy and drags it away from the palette. And never place text on a photo without one, however dark that particular photo looks — the next photo in the slot will not be.

Four states, always all four

The failed state is not an edge case here. He will open this on patchy mobile data in a lane. Every screen must remain fully usable with zero images loaded — which means an image may illustrate an instruction but must never be the instruction.

What we photograph, and what we never do

This is a design rule, not only an ethical one. We tell Aklavya where he must not raise his camera; if our own card shows that exact photograph, the instruction is dead and so is his trust in the rest of it. Our imagery has to obey the etiquette it teaches.

×Never

  • Cremation at Manikarnika or Harishchandra — it is prohibited there, and we are the ones telling him so
  • Faces at prayer, without asked-for consent
  • Children
  • Interiors where photography is barred
  • Grief, poverty, or a body, as atmosphere

✓Always

  • Named consent for a maker’s portrait — their name runs beside their face
  • Food shot as it is served, not styled
  • Alt text that carries the same information as the picture
  • A caption that says where, so the photo is guidance and not decoration
Weight budget. Under 180KB per image, AVIF with a WebP fallback, lazy below the fold, one priority image per screen at most. His battery is his lifeline — an image-heavy screen that drains it is a safety problem, not a performance one.

07 · Rules

Do, and do not

✓Do

  • Separate surfaces with soft warm shadow
  • Give exactly one action per screen the hot colour
  • Add a hairline border in the field register
  • Use the serif once per screen, for the guide’s voice
  • Put money and times in mono, tabular
  • Reserve every image slot at a fixed ratio
  • Design both themes at the same time

×Don’t

  • Fence a card with a hard border outside the field
  • Reintroduce gold anywhere but the logo
  • Put Devanagari where meaning depends on it
  • Animate anything in the field register
  • Add a second accent colour — ever
  • Tint a face or a plate of food
  • Put text on a photo without a scrim
  • Ship one theme intending to add the other later

The system is not the palette or the radius scale. It is the ladder — find the lowest failing tier, and fix only that. Everything here is downstream of that one instruction.
