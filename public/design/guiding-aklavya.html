Guiding Aklavya

He is not here to browse Varanasi. He is here to be told what to do next — from the night he books until the night he gets home.

Every screen below answers one question for one anxious first-timer. Anything that serves a browser, a gift-giver, a diaspora reader, or a collector is cut — not deferred, cut.

Who we are building for

One man, and eleven questions

“He does not know the streets, the customs, or where to begin.”

First-time visitor to Varanasi. No prior knowledge of the city. Arrives as a tourist and stays anxious until someone answers the things below — in order. He cannot feel the ghats at dawn while he is still wondering whether the man following him is a threat.

  • 02Is it safe · what scams

The structural claim

His ladder is the architecture

Maslow's tiers and the trip's timeline are the same line. Read it bottom-up, the way he lives it. Tiers one to three must be cleared before tiers four and five can land at all — which is exactly why the three storytelling segments never worked as the front door.

Bites before he books, and every single day.

The Lifeline cuts across all five. Being lost is not a tier — it happens at any height, at any hour. So it is never a page. It is always on screen.

What the application becomes

Three things, not seven

Tiers one to three collapse into one surface. Tiers four and five collapse into another. The lifeline is the third. That is the entire product.

Tiers 1–3

The Ground

Everything that must be true before he can feel anything. Stay, money, transport, SIM, water, scams, etiquette. Written once for the city, not per trip. Unglamorous and non-negotiable.

Tiers 4–5

The Days

His trip, hour by hour, each stop carrying the why only someone who loves the city knows. This is where the three segments survive — as texture inside a day, never as a doorway.

All tiers, always

The Lifeline

A named human, one tap, no menu. This is what he is actually paying for: not paper, not an app. The certainty that he cannot end up stranded.

The four screens

Ask, write, walk, call

He gets one thing at a time. A menu handed to a confused person is his confusion handed back to him.

The data model

One atom replaces six

Today there are six content shapes — Story, Food, Artisan, ItineraryStop, GuidePage (thirteen kinds), Product — all of them different ways of saying “a thing in Varanasi.” Aklavya needs one.

// The whole application is assembly over this one shape. type Stop = { name: string // "Manikarnika Ghat" kind: "place" | "eat" | "maker" | "move" | "rest" when: string // "05:40" · "before sunrise" minutes: number why: string // TIER 5 — the guiding. What only // someone who loves it would tell you. how: string // TIER 2 — from the last stop cost: string // TIER 2 — "~₹150, agree first" tip: string // one thing, never a list lost: string // ← "if you're confused here…" etiquette?: string // TIER 3 — at the stop, not in a manual photo?: { ok: boolean; note: string } // TIER 4 buy?: { what; where; fair: string } // TIER 4 coords?: [number, number] } Day = { n, theme, stops: Stop[] } Ground = { stay, money, transport, sim, water, safety, etiquette } Plan = his answers + Ground + Day[]

Two things worth naming. First: kind is where Galiyan, Swaad and Hriday go. They stop being navigation and become a tag on a stop — they survive as texture, and he never has to learn their names to use the product. Second: lost does not exist anywhere in the codebase today, and it is the highest-value field in the model. It is the entire difference between an itinerary and a guide.

stop Kashi Vishwanath, 09:00 why … lost You'll be told to leave your phone before entering. That part is real — there are official deposit counters at the gate. Anyone approaching you outside offering "VIP darshan" for a fee is not official. Keep walking. You do not need them. note Placeholder shape only. Every `lost` string must come from Phase A field research — this is content, and content is the product.

The cut

What dies, and why

Each of these serves a real person. None of them is Aklavya.

  • The India map · 12 Jyotirlinga chapterServes a browser. He is going to one city.
  • Delhi, Jaipur, Kolkata, MumbaiFour cities of content while Varanasi's own backlog sits at 0 of 19.
  • /varanasi/galiyan · /parampara · /hridayHe does not know what those words mean.
  • /cart · build-a-box · box-3d · hriday-shopServes a gift-giver. Already parked in the plan.
  • The newsletterServes an audience. He wants a trip, not a subscription.
  • /maps, standaloneA map with no question attached to it.
  • Prisma: City · Story · Food · ArtisanModels a catalogue. Nothing reads it today anyway.
  • The 5 / 8 / 14-day book tabsHe has one trip length. Ask him — don't offer tabs.
  • The three-doorway hub pageA choice he is not equipped to make.

And the sidebar goes first. A first-timer handed a menu of twelve cities and three untranslated segment names has been handed his own confusion back, in a nicer font.

Nothing here is deleted from history — /internal stays as your own instrument, and the researched craft and food content becomes Stop records rather than being thrown away. What dies is their status as destinations.

Sequence

Finish one thing to done

Ordered so that a guided soul is possible before most of the code exists.

  1. 01
    The Door and The Asking

    Captures a soul with zero content behind it. Two screens, a form, a WhatsApp field. Shippable this week.

  2. 02
    Write one plan by hand

    For Soul #001, the real traveller who already asked. You are the guide. The plan is the product; the app is the wrapper around it.

  3. 03
    /plan renders that plan

    Now the thing you wrote by hand becomes reusable. The Stop shape gets proven against real content, not invented ahead of it.

  4. 04
    Fill lost for the dozen places first-timers actually get confused

    Phase A field research. The queue, the deposit counter, the boat price, the wood donation, the lane after dark. This is the moat.

  5. 05
    The Ground

    Stay, money, transport, SIM, water, safety, etiquette. Written once. Tiers one to three, finally owned by something.

  6. 06
    /now — the field register

    Only after a real traveller has walked a plan and told you where it broke. Building this first would be guessing.

Steps 02 and 04 are the actual product and neither is a coding task. The application is perhaps a fifth of this work — which is the honest reason the current build looks finished and still guides nobody.

The test for every screen, every field, and every cut: does this make one anxious first-timer feel that someone is walking beside him? If the answer needs a paragraph, it is a no.
