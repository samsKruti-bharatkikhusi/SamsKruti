# SamsKruti — Product Experience Concept

_The north-star document. Everything we build for a segment is judged against this._

## The one idea
**The hero of the platform is the ADDITION, not the product.**

A Banarasi saree is not the point. The *why* behind it — who wove it, why the
motif curves the way it does, what disappears when the last weaver is gone — that
is the point. Every product is a portal to a story. We sell civilizational
memory, not objects.

> "Don't buy a product. Carry a piece of civilizational memory."

## What "experiential" means here
Right now the page is a catalog — static, nothing is alive. The goal is the
opposite: the user should feel they have *entered* the city.

- Not "here are products." Instead: "here is why this exists, here is the hand
  that made it, here is where in the city you'd find it."
- The user should feel: _"I didn't just look at Varanasi — I entered its heart."_
- Exploratory, animated, step-by-step — discovery, not a product list.

## The two ways to buy
1. **Ready-made boxes** — the curated Heritage Gift Boxes (buy directly).
2. **Create your own box** — the user *builds* a box by adding products. The
   feeling of curation is the experience: as items are added, the box fills,
   images appear, and each addition carries its story. The act of building the
   box — "the addition" — is the hero moment.

## Per product, three layers
For every curated product we hold:
1. **The product** — image(s), name, craft, GI status, price band.
2. **The addition** — the image/moment of it going into the box; the curated act.
3. **The why / history** — why this product exists, the people who make it, the
   lineage, what it means to the city. This is the soul.

## Curation, not volume
- **Not 50 products.** Pick ~**10–15 flagship products** for the segment.
- Go deep on each: history, the maker, the location, the imagery.
- We acknowledge real artisan stories aren't all documented yet — we will
  **craft plausible, respectful stories** and **generate/source images** to
  bring the concept alive, then replace with real research over time.

## The map is part of the product
On the city boundary map (the dotted outline), show **where each product is
found** — the lanes, quarters, and bazaars. The map answers "where does this
come from," tying each product to a real place in the city. The cart/box can
reflect what's been gathered from across the city.

## Scope discipline
- Build **one segment fully** (starting with **Sheher Ka Hriday**) before moving
  to the next.
- Other pages/screens stay as-is for now; core concept is locked.
- Cart/checkout mechanics are deferred — first make it experiential.
