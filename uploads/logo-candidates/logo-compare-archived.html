The measurement that decides it. C is 99.7% warm. Its blue is desaturated so far that it does not register as a hue at all — it reads as a cool grey, which the earth palette accommodates without complaint. B was 93.1% warm with a real blue. So C is not a compromise between A and B: on the palette rule it is as compliant as A, while carrying far more story.

The unexpected result. C may hold better than A at small size, not worse. In A the S is described by soft tonal shifts, which are the first thing to vanish when an image is downscaled. 06 · The call

What I’d choose

F · Earth road

F. It is the monochrome, with the flaw fixed.

A was eliminated for one reason: at nav size it retained the least structure of any candidate (37.0). Its S was drawn in soft tonal shifts, and tonal shifts are what downscaling destroys.

F is the same palette with that flaw repaired. Identical saturation to A at 32% — equally calm beside the button — but the dark brown bands give it genuine light-against-dark structure, and it carries 19% more of the mark to 44px (43.9 vs 37.0). It has zero cool hues, it is the only candidate that is 100% warm and as quiet as the original, and it shows a cobbled path winding through the frame.

C remains the legibility champion at 55.5, and if the mark had to survive at 16px above all else, C would win. But it buys that with 48% saturation and olive-and-slate landscape colour, which makes it a second colourful object next to the one element that must always be loudest.

D and E are out. Both are calm and warm, but D dropped the path entirely, and E’s path is pale cream against cream — it looks like a road at 512px and disappears by 64px, which is the worst of both.

F is also the smallest file of the six at 40KB, which is a small thing that says a true thing: it has the least redundant detail.
